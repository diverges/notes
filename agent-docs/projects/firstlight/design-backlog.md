# First Light: Unselected design candidates

## 2026-09-28: Candidates separated from the authoritative GDD

The user requested a GDD that describes the game directly, without decision labels or approval history. Revision 0.9 removes mixed-status examples rather than silently treating them as settled rules. The following earlier candidates remain available for discussion; they are not requirements or authorization to implement.

### Survival and persistence

The earlier draft suggested Energy, Warmth and Nourishment on 0–100 scales: travel/work spend energy, rest restores it, cold increases costs, and food improves recovery. It suggested warnings at 25 energy, stopping repeated work at 10, and collapse at zero. Recovery returned the player to a rest site, retaining equipment, knowledge, skills and structures while placing raw supplies in a marked recoverable bundle with no prototype expiry. Separate thirst, injury and disease systems were excluded from that example, not permanently ruled out.

Persistence candidates included no offline decay, saving after completed changes and Save and quit, visible save errors, and preserving inventory, skills, discoveries, station state, structures, resource depletion and terrain responses. The current skill engine has no pending station output. These choices still need a dedicated design. Settings/focus pause and live Bag/Skills are established behavior and remain in the GDD.

### Recipes, storage and content

Candidate recipes: sharp stone + branch + string at a workbench makes a stone hatchet; two clay at a pottery bench makes an unfired bowl, then one fuel at a kiln fires it. String could bind tools or repair shelter. Earlier examples used separate station collection; that does not match the current direct-output skill engine. A chest extending storage while crafting draws only from the pack was an idea, not an implemented rule.

The old content estimate was eight raw material types, six recipes, a flax patch, clay patch, landmark, shelter, wheel, workbench, chest, fire, pottery bench and kiln. The new forest opening grants none of these as possessions and does not establish a settlement beside the player. Flax gathering/renewal and station discovery order still need definition. Earlier 10–15-minute opening and 10–20-minute session targets were untested and need reconsideration against the user's slow, grindy direction.

### Skills and combat

The six skills beyond Woodcutting are named but their mechanics remain open. Earlier ideas associated Cooking with meals/preservation, Smithing with metalworking/repair, Strength with force/heavy tasks, Vitality with health/recovery, and Agility with traversal. Weapon Mastery was deferred while combat remains unarmed. Example benefits and practice sources were not approved activity designs.

Five rank names (Novice, Practiced, Capable, Skilled, Master) with practice plus milestones were suggestions for other skills, not Woodcutting gates. Examples included two different meals, forging/repair, strenuous work, surviving an expedition and traversal challenges. Punching might award Strength later; it currently grants no XP. Do not infer implemented ranks, milestones or skill effects from these candidates.

### Building, interface and delivery

The player has a 1x1 footprint and terrain responds to building placement automatically. Flooring as 1x1 pieces and one-tile wall segments were sizing examples; wall-edge occupancy, furnace dimensions, passage widths, removal and save/load still need specification. Current ground movement is continuous, not established as tile-stepped.

Mobile drawer/action sheets, two-finger camera gestures, landscape orientation, remapping, scalable text, drag alternatives and camera-motion adjustments were accessibility/control candidates. There is no touch or accessibility validation yet. The old six-color mood table was a preliminary scene-role suggestion; the accepted forty-color texture is now the GDD palette reference.

### Historical evidence

Implementation changes and test results remain in specs 000–004, the Play Mode guide and dated project context. Older storyboards and UI captures remain in the visual guide and image files. Main-GDD editorial cleanup does not change their historical verification or game behavior.
